# ReInsPIRe Improvements (Gen 1, Gen 2, Gen 3 & Gen 4)

All online, offline, and wire optimizations across **Gen 1** (`I5, I3a, I3b, I4`), **Gen 2** (`I1, I2, I3, I4`), **Gen 3** (`S3, S4, S5a`, grouped selectors, continuous cross-colour response packing), and **Gen 4** (`R1, R1a, R1b, R2`, and 2D row-stripe parallelism for tall colour tables $R_i > 2048$), classified into three tiers: **(1) Algorithmic & Transform-Domain**, **(2) Arithmetic Radix & Cache/SIMD Kernels**, and **(3) Wire Serialization, Modulus Switching & Sampling**. Detailed benchmarks and 3-way comparisons are in [`results.md`](./results.md); the correctness, noise, and security audit is in [`audit.md`](./audit.md).

## Class 1: Algorithmic & Transform-Domain Improvements

1. **Hoisted LWE-to-RLWE Packing Transforms (`Gen 1 I3a` — `crypto/lwes_to_rlwe.cc`, `crypto/pir/server/pir_server.cc`)**:
   - `FinalizeMatrixPack` computes $\langle \mathbf{t}_{\text{vec}, h}, \mathbf{y}_{\text{vec}, h} \rangle$ via a chunked negacyclic FFT inner product. Because $\mathbf{t}_{\text{vec}, h}$ depends only on the database/CRS and $\mathbf{y}_{\text{vec}, h}$ is shared across all partitions and columns of a request, precomputing `FFT(t_vec_h)` offline (`PrecomputeTVecHFfts`) and `FFT(y_vec_h)` once per request eliminates all 8 per-call forward FFTs.
   - A multi-color request (Tiptoe adapter, `crypto/pir/benchmarking/tiptoe_workload.cc`) calls `ProcessResponse` once per color, and every color shares the packing key. The adapter therefore computes `FFT(y_vec_h)` once per request and passes it to every call (optional `y_vec_h_fft` argument), which removes $P - 1$ identical transforms. Its time still counts in `server_pack_finalize_ms` and `server_packing_ms`.
2. **Hermitian-Folded `TwoDigitsFft` & Paired Real IFFT (`Gen 2 I2a/b` — `crypto/polynomial_fft.cc`)**:
   - Packs pairs of real digit chunks into one complex vector $Z[k] = d_0[k] + i d_1[k]$, runs a single twisted forward FFT, and separates $\text{FFT}(d_0), \text{FFT}(d_1)$ in $\mathcal{O}(d)$ via conjugate symmetry $\overline{Z[d - 1 - k]}$, halving forward FFTs in `ToChunkedFft` and offline preprocessing.
   - Dually, packs pairs of real output accumulators as $A[k] + i B[k]$ to recover both polynomials from a single inverse FFT (`2` IFFTs instead of `3` per packed ciphertext). Combined with `I3a`, reduces per-call transforms in `FinalizeMatrixPack` from **11 FFTs to 2 IFFTs**.
3. **Length-$d$ Twisted Negacyclic FFTs in `FinalizeMatrixPack` (`Gen 3 S4c` — `crypto/polynomial_fft.cc`, `crypto/lwes_to_rlwe.cc`)**:
   - Replaces the length-$2d = 4096$ zero-padded real FFTs (`ctx.real_plan`) and post-IFFT cyclic wrap-around subtraction (`out[j] - out[j + d]`) in `TwoDigitsFft`, `ToChunkedFft`, `InnerProductFft`, and `RecombineDigitProducts` with **length-$d = 2048$ twisted negacyclic FFTs** (`ctx.negacyclic_plan` with root $\psi = e^{i\pi/d}$).
   - Cuts the offline precomputed `t_vec_h_ffts_` footprint in half (`256 KiB` vs `512 KiB` per column), halves the frequency-domain complex FMA loop (`2048` vs `4096` complex points), and reduces `server_pack_finalize_ms` by **52%–65%** while producing **bit-for-bit identical** polynomial coefficients.
4. **Twisted-FFT Secret Key Cache & Pre-Modswitched CRS Mask (`Gen 1 I4` + `Gen 2 I1b` — `crypto/polynomial_fft.cc`, `crypto/encryption.cc`)**:
   - Caches the ternary secret key $\mathbf{s} \in \{-1, 0, 1\}^d$ once in a twisted $d$-point `f64` negacyclic FFT domain (`FftPolynomial`). Because $u + v + \log_2 d \le 44$ bits (`kFftExactProductBits`) guarantees exact integer recovery after `nearbyint`, the modulus-switched response decrypts in a **single unchunked `f64` transform** (replacing 52-bit NTT multiplications).
   - Pre-rescaling the deterministic CRS mask $\mathbf{a}_{\text{resp}}$ from $q=2^{52}$ to $q_1$ once at setup (`a_mod_switched_t1_`) eliminates online mask rescaling during both server `ModSwitch` and client `Decrypt`.

## Class 2: Arithmetic Radix & Cache/SIMD Kernel Optimizations

1. **$\mathbb{Z}_{2^{64}}$ $2^{32}$-Radix Split & 6-Row Blocked Packing Matrix (`Gen 1 I5` + `Gen 2 I4a/b` — `crypto/matrix.cc`)**:
   - Splits the 64-bit vector once per call as $y = y_{\text{lo}} + 2^{32} y_{\text{hi}} \pmod{2^{64}}$ with $y_{\text{lo}} = \text{int32}(y \bmod 2^{32})$ and $y_{\text{hi}} = (y \gg 32) + \text{bit}_{31}(y)$ (canceling sign extension), replacing emulated 64-bit SIMD multiplies with native 32-bit `MulEven` ($32\times 32\to 64$) + `Mul` ($32\times 32\to 32$).
   - Gen 2 `I4` interleaves `(y_lo, y_hi)` into a single `y_packed` stream and unrolls **6 matrix rows** per loop (12 YMM accumulators in 16 YMM registers), cutting vector L1 load traffic by `33%` and streaming the 128 MiB packing matrix at the single-core DRAM ceiling (~25 GB/s).
2. **Sign-Flipped `int16_t` DB & 16-Lane AVX2 `vpmaddwd` Contraction Kernel (`Gen 1 I3b` + `Gen 2 I3` + `Gen 3 S3b` — `crypto/matrix.cc`)**:
   - Gen 1 `I3b` and Gen 2 `I3` introduced the 6-row blocked database kernel with next-row-block software prefetching (`hwy::Prefetch`).
   - Gen 3 `S3b` sign-flips the condensed `uint16_t` database entries once offline during `Matrix<uint16_t>::Condense`:
     $$D'_{r,j} = D_{r,j} - 2^{15} \in [-32768, 32767]$$
     and decomposes each centered query coefficient $v_r \in [-2^{39}, 2^{39}] \cdot 2^{12}$ (or general 52-bit $v_r$ decomposed into 3 signed balanced 14-bit digits $d_0, d_1, d_2 \in [-8192, 8192]$ with $v_r \equiv d_0 + d_1 2^{14} + d_2 2^{28}$).
   - Pairs of rows $(2r, 2r+1)$ are multiplied and accumulated into 32-bit signed integers in a single **16-lane AVX2 `_mm256_madd_epi16` (`vpmaddwd`)** instruction (4× the lane density of 64-bit `pmuludq`), with exact 32-bit overflow safety ($\lvert -32768 \cdot 8192 \rvert \times 2 \times 1024 = 2^{29} \cdot 2 = 2^{30} < 2^{31}-1$) and a single hoisted scalar bias restoration $32768 \sum_r v_r \pmod{2^{52}}$ per output row.
3. **AVX2+FMA Stage-Twiddle Butterfly Kernel (`Gen 2 I2c` — `crypto/fft.h`)**:
   - Precomputes contiguous per-stage twiddle tables and executes 2 complex butterflies per 256-bit `__m256d` FMA with dedicated twiddle-free radix-2/radix-4 initial stages.

## Class 3: Wire Serialization, Modulus Switching & Sampling

1. **128-Bit Word-Stream Bit-Packing & Headerless Wire (`Gen 2 I1a` + `Gen 3 S4b` — `crypto/polynomial.cc`, `crypto/pir/benchmarking/tiptoe_workload.cc`, `crypto/pir/server/pir_server.cc`)**:
   - Gen 2 `I1a` replaced bit-by-bit loops in `ToProto`/`CreateFromProto` with a `uint128_t` word accumulator flushing/reading 64-bit words.
   - Gen 3 `S4b` removes redundant 32-bit length prefixes from fixed-schema wire messages (`PackCoeffsAppend`/`UnpackCoeffs`) and pre-packs the static modulus-switched response mask `a_mod_switched_t1_packed_` (`6,400 B` per column) once at server setup so online `ProcessResponse(..., /*skip_a_t1=*/true)` skips `ct.a` bit-packing entirely (`-45% to -56%` `server_encode_ms`).
2. **Divisionless Power-of-Two `Rescale` & Single-Rounding $q_1 = 2^{25}$ Decryption (`Gen 2 I1c` + `Gen 3 S4a` — `crypto/polynomial.cc`, `crypto/encryption.cc`)**:
   - Gen 2 `I1c` replaced 128-bit software divisions (`__udivti3`) by powers of two with `(x + round_offset) >> shift`.
   - Gen 3 `S4a` eliminates the intermediate rounding of $a_{\text{sw}} \cdot s$ from $q_1$ to $q_2 = 2^{18}$ in `DecryptAfterModulusSwitchImpl`: instead of rounding twice ($q \to q_1 \to q_2 \to p$), it left-shifts $b_{\text{sw}}$ by $\log_2(q_1/q_2) = 7$ bits into $\mathbb{Z}_{q_1}$, computes $(a_{\text{sw}} \cdot s + (b_{\text{sw}} \ll 7)) \bmod q_1$, and rounds **once** from $q_1 = 2^{25}$ to $p = 2^{16}$. This eliminates the $\sigma_{\text{mid}}^2 = \frac{d}{6} = 341.33\text{ LSB}_{q_2}^2$ intermediate rounding variance, allowing $\log_2 q_1$ to drop from `29` to `25` (**`-1,024 B` per response ciphertext** AND **`-20% to -22%` lower total decryption noise**).
3. **40-Bit First-Dimension Selector Modulus Switching (`Gen 3 S3a` — `crypto/pir/benchmarking/tiptoe_workload.cc`)**:
   - Modulus-switches each first-dimension selector coefficient from $q = 2^{52}$ to $q_{\text{sel}} = 2^{40}$ (`y_sw = (y + 2^11) >> 12`, `5 B/coeff` vs `6.5 B/coeff`, **`-23.1%` selector upload bytes**) and rescales back by `<< 12` on server unpack. Because $q/q_{\text{sel}} = 2^{12}$ aligns with the `vpmaddwd` kernel's 40-bit centered query input, the added selector rounding variance at $q_2 = 2^{18}$ is only $\le 0.16\text{ LSB}_{q_2}^2$ at $M=2048$ (over $2000\times$ smaller than the $341.33\text{ LSB}_{q_2}^2$ saved by `S4a`).
4. **Bit-Sliced `popcount` Centered Binomial & Ternary Samplers + Lock-Free `FastChaChaPrng` (`Gen 3 S5a` — `crypto/encryption.cc`, `crypto/pir/benchmarking/tiptoe_workload.cc`)**:
   - Replaces `shell_encryption`'s `SampleFromErrorDistribution<MontgomeryInt<Uint32>>` (which allocated a `MontgomeryInt` vector and called `Rand8()` 20 times per coefficient) with `SampleCenteredBinomialAndSubtract`, which draws `5` `Rand64()` words per `2` coefficients (`160` bits/coeff for $\sigma^2 = 40$) and computes $\text{popcount}(x) - \text{popcount}(y) - (a \cdot s)_i \bmod q$ directly in registers.
   - Replaces `SampleFromUniformTernary<MontgomeryInt<Uint32>>` in `SampleSecretKey` with direct bitsliced rejection sampling into `CoeffType`, and replaces mutex-locked `::rlwe::ChaChaPrng` with single-threaded `FastChaChaPrng`, speeding up `client_query_ms` by **`3.2×–3.5×`**.
5. **Persistent Server `FftContext` & Direct `CreateCondensed<int32_t>` (`Gen 2 I2d`, `I4c` — `crypto/pir/server/pir_server.cc`)**:
   - Reuses a per-server `fft_ctx_` workspace instead of allocating `320 KiB` per `FinalizeMatrixPack` call, and constructs `combined_pack_matrix_` directly from column-major `pack_matrices` without an intermediate `Matrix::Transpose()` allocation.

## Multicore Online Server (`PIR_THREAD_CPUS`, Tiptoe adapter)

`crypto/pir/benchmarking/tiptoe_workload.cc` can answer each request on several pinned threads. With `PIR_THREAD_CPUS` unset it behaves as before: one online thread, pinned by `PIR_ONLINE_CPU` if set, and `PIR_PREPROCESS_THREADS` offline jobs; the new setup fields then describe a single task (T = 1), and query records are unchanged.

- **`PIR_THREAD_CPUS=c0,c1,...`**: one worker per listed logical CPU, so T = number of entries. The ids must be unique and inside the process affinity mask. The variable cannot be combined with `PIR_ONLINE_CPU`, and `PIR_PREPROCESS_THREADS` is ignored. Invalid settings exit nonzero with a message.
- **Decomposition**: after unified block layout (`N_resp = ceil(P * d_row / 2048)` response blocks and tallest physical colour `M_max`), tasks decompose in 2D across $G = \min(N_{\text{resp}}, T)$ contiguous block groups and $S = \min(\lceil M_{\max} / 2048 \rceil, \lfloor T / G \rfloor)$ `2048`-row-aligned Stage 1 row stripes (`RowStripe` in `crypto/pir/pir_params.h`). Task $(g, s)$ runs on worker $w = g S + s$, pinned to `c_w`. Worker 0 is the main thread, which also runs the client. Workers persist across requests, and CPUs beyond the $G S$ tasks stay idle.
- **Owner computes**: each worker pins itself, then preprocesses its owned `(blocks, stripe)` slice via `PreprocessCrossColorStripe` so its condensed Stage 1 database and (for `s = 0`) Stage 2 packing matrices reside on the NUMA node of the CPU that streams them. Stripe `s = 0` owns the summed offline LWE hints and `PreprocessMatrixPackOutput` for its block range `[p0, p1)`; stripes `s > 0` hold Stage 1 `CrossColorDbSlice` matrices only (`ComputeStage1`). Every server, with its `FftContext` and timers, is used by a single thread.
- **Per request**: the main thread decodes the key bundle and selectors and computes `FFT(y_vec_h)` once (see Class 1). When $S = 1$, each worker runs `ProcessResponse` + wire serialization in a single barrier. When $S > 1$, Phase 1 runs `ComputeStage1` across all $(g, s)$ workers in parallel; Phase 2 runs on the `s = 0` workers, summing `partial[g * S + s]` modulo $2^{64}$ into `s = 0`'s accumulator and calling `PackFromStage1` + wire serialization. Because ring addition modulo $2^{64}$ is associative and commutative, the serialized response is **bit-for-bit identical** between `T = 1` and `T = 32`.
- **Records**: the setup record reports `online_threads` (T), `online_cpus`, `block_groups` ($G$), `stripes` ($S$), `responses` ($N_{\text{resp}}$), `bundle_rows`, `selector_bits`, `centered_db`, and `tasks` (`worker`, `cpu`, half-open `blocks` range, and `stripe`). In threaded mode `offline_threads` is the number of tasks. In query records, `server_ms` is the main thread's wall time from request decode to serialized response. The `server_*_ms` phase fields follow the critical path, and threaded mode adds `server_cpu_ms`, the busy server time summed over all workers.
- **Placement**: one thread per physical core (SMT siblings slow requests down), spread over both NUMA nodes (`pack_matrix` streams 32 MiB per (color, column), so DRAM bandwidth per node is the limit), and preferably not CPU 0.

### Non-Power-of-Two Colour Tables (Tiptoe adapter)

- **Change (`crypto/pir/benchmarking/tiptoe_workload.cc:237`)**: Relaxed the workload header check from `Check(m > 0 && m <= 2048 && !(m & (m - 1)), "Unsupported M")` to `Check(m > 0 && m <= 2048, "Unsupported M")`. The core ReInsPIRe library already accepts any $1 \le R_i \le 2048$ when $t = 1$ (`pir_params.cc:35-55` only requires $t \mid \text{num\_entries}$).
- **Wire & work formulas ($R_i$ = real rows of colour $i$, vs padded $M_i = 2^{\lceil \log_2 R_i \rceil}$)**:
  | Quantity | Formula ($R_i$ = real rows) |
  |---|---|
  | **Upload** | $53{,}248 + 5 \sum_{i=0}^{P-1} R_i$ bytes (was $53{,}248 + 5 \sum M_i$; wire granularity is **1 row = 5 B**) |
  | **Download** | $11{,}008 \cdot P \cdot C$ bytes (**unchanged**) |
  | **Online DB MACs** | $2048 \cdot C \cdot \sum_{i=0}^{P-1} \text{round\_up}(R_i, 32)$ (**32-row server granularity**) |
  | **Packing & offline hint** | $2 \cdot 2048^2 \cdot P \cdot C$ online packing MACs; offline hint pads to $2048$ (**unchanged**) |
- **32-row server granularity**: `PreprocessingServer` zero-pads database matrix columns to a multiple of 32 for the 16-lane AVX2 `uint16_t`/`int16_t` contraction kernel (`preprocessing_server.h:135-154`), and `PirServer::ProcessResponse` zero-pads the unpacked $R_i$-coefficient query vector to match (`pir_server.cc:266-277`), while the offline hint always zero-pads columns to $d = 2048$ (`preprocessing_server.h:232-246`).
- **Measured effect**:
  | Geometry | `T=1` Server | `T=32` Server | Upload Bytes |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | `2×1311, C=29` | **−15%** | noise | `73,728 → 66,358 B` (`-10.0%`, DB MACs `-35.9%`) |
  | `38×1300, C=1` | **−14%** | **−11%** | `442,368 → 300,248 B` (`-32.1%`) |
  | `11×239` and `38×2000` | noise | noise | small drop (`5 B` per unpadded row) |
  Because LWE-to-RLWE packing dominates online server time and is independent of $R_i$, the server latency speedup is modest while upload decreases by $5(M_i - R_i)$ bytes per colour.
- **Unchanged correctness, noise & security**:
  - **Correctness**: Verified across $R_i \in \{1, 2, 17, 31, 32, 33, 63, 64, 65, 96, 97, 239, 874, 1000, 1311, 2016, 2017, 2047, 2048\}$, mixed heights, $C \in \{1, 2, 3, 5, 29\}$, $T \in \{1, 4, 8, 16, 32\}$, boundary targets $0$ and $R_i - 1$, a 100-query stress run, and clean rejection of $M = 0$ and $M = 2049$.
  - **Noise (bit-identical download)**: With the client seed fixed, the serialized download for real-row tables ($R_i$) is **100% bit-for-bit identical** to the power-of-two padded tables ($M_i$) across both single-threaded and multi-threaded execution (padding rows are zero and contribute $0 \bmod 2^{64}$, and the hint is identical). Covered by `PirEndToEndTest::TiptoeNonPowerOfTwoDownloadIsBitIdenticalToPaddedAndThreaded` in `crypto/pir/pir_end_to_end_real_params_test.cc`.
  - **Security**: The uploaded selector is the first $R_i$ coefficients of the same 2048-coefficient RLWE ciphertext under the unchanged `SelectorSeed(slot)` CRS, packing key bundle, packing seed, and hint—a public projection of the padded upload. Revealing $R_i$ via upload length (`5·R_i`) leaks nothing new because table populations are already public layout metadata.

### Whole-Colour Grouped Selector Encryption (`CreateGroupedSelector`, `BuildSelectorGroupPlans`, `RotateGroupMaskForInterval`)

When colour table heights satisfy $R_i < d = 2048$, encrypting a separate degree-2048 RLWE selector per colour wastes $d - R_i$ coefficients of each RLWE sample. Whole-colour grouped selector encryption packs multiple consecutive colours into a single degree-$d$ RLWE encryption on the client while leaving the wire format and online server completely unchanged:

- **Greedy whole-colour bin-packing (`BuildSelectorGroupPlans` in `crypto/pir/pir_params.h`)**:
  - Consecutive colours $i \in g$ with $\sum_{i \in g} R_i \le d = 2048$ are assigned disjoint contiguous coefficient intervals $[\text{offset}_i, \text{offset}_i + R_i) \subset [0, d)$, reducing client selector RLWE encryptions from $P$ to $G_{\text{sel}} \ge \lceil \sum_i R_i / 2048 \rceil$.
  - For each group $g$, `PirClient::CreateGroupedSelector` (`crypto/pir/client/pir_client.cc`) constructs a single multi-hot plaintext $m_g(X) = \sum_{i \in g} X^{\text{offset}_i + r_i^*}$ ($r_i^* \in [0, R_i)$), samples one group CRS mask $a_g(X)$ from `GroupSelectorSeed(g)`, and encrypts $m_g(X)$ under one RLWE sample $(a_g(X), b_g(X))$.
  - The client slices each colour's interval $b_g[\text{offset}_i : \text{offset}_i + R_i)$, applies the 40-bit modulus switch, and appends the `5·R_i`-byte slice to the upload stream in colour order.
- **Offline signed negacyclic mask rotation (`RotateGroupMaskForInterval` in `crypto/pir/pir_params.h`, `PreprocessingServer::Preprocess` in `crypto/pir/server/preprocessing_server.h`)**:
  - In `ComputeHintMatrix`, column $r \in [0, R_i)$ of colour $i$'s database multiplies row $\text{offset}_i + r$ of the negacyclic matrix $\text{Negacyclic}(a_g)$.
  - For any $\text{offset}_i + r < d$, row $\text{offset}_i + r$ of $\text{Negacyclic}(a_g)$ is identically row $r$ of $\text{Negacyclic}(\tilde{a}_i)$, where
    $$\tilde{a}_i(X) = X^{-\text{offset}_i} a_g(X) \bmod (X^d + 1), \qquad \tilde{a}_i[k] = \begin{cases} a_g[k + \text{offset}_i] & 0 \le k < d - \text{offset}_i, \\ -a_g[k + \text{offset}_i - d] \bmod q & d - \text{offset}_i \le k < d. \end{cases}$$
  - Rotating $a_g(X)$ by $X^{-\text{offset}_i} \bmod (X^d + 1)$ once during offline `Preprocess` allows every colour server to keep its standard $[0, R_i)$ database layout and online contraction with zero online server changes.
- **Zero cross-colour contamination & RLWE security**:
  - Because each coefficient of $b_g(X) = \Delta m_g(X) - a_g(X)s(X) + e_g(X) \bmod q$ is rounded to 40 bits independently and colour $i$'s server contracts only $b_g[\text{offset}_i : \text{offset}_i + R_i)$, entries of $m_g$ outside $[\text{offset}_i, \text{offset}_i + R_i)$ have **zero** effect on colour $i$'s query slice or response noise.

### Continuous Cross-Colour LWE Response Packing Across All Row Widths (`PackedRowWords`, `ResolveRowU16`, `PreprocessCrossColorStripe`, `PirServer::CreateCrossColor`)

Previously, `PackedRowWords(row_u16, d)` (`crypto/pir/pir_params.h:300-302`) and `tiptoe_workload.cc` (`ResolveRowU16`, `d_row`) restricted cross-colour packing to sub-half-tile rows ($C = 1$ and $d_{\text{row}} \le 1024$ words / $2{,}048\text{ B}$), rounding any wider row ($d_{\text{row}} > 1024$ or $C > 1$) up to a whole multiple of $d = 2048$ words ($4{,}096\text{ B}$) per colour ($P \cdot C$ response ciphertexts). Now `PackedRowWords(row_u16, d)` returns `row_u16` unconditionally for **all** $d_{\text{row}} \ge 1$, and `ResolveRowU16` (`tiptoe_workload.cc:72-111` — via `PIR_ROW_U16`, `client.pal` header $\lceil h \cdot m_{\text{sub}} \cdot n_{\text{bits}} / 8 \rceil$, or trailing-zero scan across `c * 2048` words) / `d_row` (`tiptoe_workload.cc:421`) resolve the exact active word width $d_{\text{row}} \in [1, 2048 C]$ across any column count $C = \lceil d_{\text{row}} / 2048 \rceil \ge 1$ — including intermediate single-tile widths ($1024 < d_{\text{row}} < 2048$), multi-tile widths ($d_{\text{row}} > 2048$), bundled rows ($B_{\text{row}} \cdot w_{16} > 1024$), and multi-column bundled rows ($C_{\text{orig}} > 1, B_{\text{row}} > 1$). All $P$ colours' $d_{\text{row}}$-word rows are packed as one flat $P \cdot d_{\text{row}}$ LWE stream into $N_{\text{resp}} = \lceil P \cdot d_{\text{row}} / 2048 \rceil$ RLWE response ciphertexts:

- **Offline continuous cross-colour block/stripe preprocessing (`PreprocessingServer::PreprocessCrossColorStripe`, `PreprocessCrossColorBlock` in `crypto/pir/server/preprocessing_server.h`)**:
  - Response block $b \in [0, N_{\text{resp}})$ covers global LWE indices $[g_0, g_1) = [b \cdot 2048, \min((b + 1) \cdot 2048, P \cdot d_{\text{row}}))$, decomposing that range into contiguous colour segments via `slot = pos / d_row`, `u0 = pos % d_row`, and `take = min(d_row - u0, g1 - pos)`.
  - Whether $d_{\text{row}} \le 1024$, $1024 < d_{\text{row}} < 2048$, or $d_{\text{row}} > 2048$ (where a single colour spans multiple consecutive $2048$-word blocks with `take = 2048` on interior blocks and `take < 2048` on shared boundary blocks), `PreprocessCrossColorStripe` transposes and condenses only the `take` active Stage 1 `int16_t` rows `[u0, u0 + take)` of colour `slot` (`CrossColorDbSlice`) and computes only their `take` LWE hint polynomials under colour `slot`'s selector CRS mask.
  - The assembled 2048-row hint block (summed modulo $2^{64}$ across stripes when $S > 1$) is passed once to `PreprocessPacking` under the shared `packing_seed`, producing a single `PreprocessMatrixPackOutput` for response block $b$.
- **Online cross-colour server & client unpack (`PirServer::CreateCrossColor` in `crypto/pir/server/pir_server.cc`, `tiptoe_workload.cc`)**:
  - Online, `ComputeStage1` multiplies each `CrossColorDbSlice` against `(*request.color_queries)[sl.color]` to populate `b_prime_all` ($2048$ coefficients total across slices), followed by one `PackFromStage1` ($2048 \times 4096$ `pack_matrix` multiply + `FinalizeMatrixPack` + modulus switch) per response block.
  - The client decrypts the $N_{\text{resp}}$ RLWE response ciphertexts and scatters each segment `(slot = idx / d_row, off_in_row = idx % d_row, take = min(d_row - off_in_row, end - idx))` back into `recovered[slot][off_in_row : off_in_row + take)`.
- **Wire & work formulas (all $d_{\text{row}} \ge 1$, $C = \lceil d_{\text{row}} / 2048 \rceil$, $N_{\text{resp}} = \lceil P \cdot d_{\text{row}} / 2048 \rceil$)**:
  | Quantity | Whole-Tile Per-Colour ($P \cdot C$ blocks) | Continuous Cross-Colour ($N_{\text{resp}}$ blocks) |
  |---|---|---|
  | **Upload** | $53{,}248 + \sum_{i=0}^{P-1} \lceil M_{i,\text{phys}} \cdot b_{\text{sel}} / 8 \rceil$ B | $53{,}248 + \sum_{i=0}^{P-1} \lceil M_{i,\text{phys}} \cdot b_{\text{sel}} / 8 \rceil$ B (**unchanged**) |
  | **Download** | $11{,}008 \cdot P \cdot C$ B | $11{,}008 \cdot N_{\text{resp}}$ B (**reduced by $N_{\text{resp}} / (P C)$**) |
  | **Condensed Stage 1 DB RAM & Online MACs** | $2048 C \sum_{i=0}^{P-1} \text{round\_up}(M_{i,\text{phys}}, 32)$ | $d_{\text{row}} \sum_{i=0}^{P-1} \text{round\_up}(M_{i,\text{phys}}, 32)$ (**reduced by $d_{\text{row}} / (2048 C)$**) |
  | **Offline Hint FFTs & Packing Setup** | $P \cdot C$ full 2048-word blocks | $N_{\text{resp}}$ blocks (**reduced by $N_{\text{resp}} / (P C)$**) |
  | **Online Stage 2 Packing MACs & FFTs** | $2 \cdot 2048^2 \cdot P \cdot C$ | $2 \cdot 2048^2 \cdot N_{\text{resp}}$ (**reduced by $N_{\text{resp}} / (P C)$**) |
  | **Client Decryptions** | $P \cdot C$ RLWE decryptions | $N_{\text{resp}}$ RLWE decryptions (**reduced by $N_{\text{resp}} / (P C)$**) |
- **Concrete savings across previously un-packed row widths ($d_{\text{row}} > 1024$)**:
  - **`4,128 B` rows (`d_row = 2064` words, $C = 2$) at $P = 128$**: Whole-tile padding required $P \cdot C = 256$ response ciphertexts (`2,818,048 B` download); continuous cross-colour packing uses $N_{\text{resp}} = \lceil 128 \cdot 2064 / 2048 \rceil = 129$ ciphertexts (`1,420,032 B` download) — a **`-49.6%` reduction** in response download, Stage 2 `LWEsToRLWE` packing, offline `PreprocessPacking`, and client decrypts, plus **`-49.6%`** (`2064 / 4096`) fewer Stage 1 MACs, condensed DB RAM bytes, and offline hint FFTs.
  - **`3,072 B` rows (`d_row = 1536` words, $C = 1$, e.g. `W1`)**: Cuts response ciphertexts from $P \to \lceil 3P / 4 \rceil$ (e.g., $32 \to 24$ cts on `W1` at $M = 2048, P = 32$, `716.0 → 630.0 KiB` total wire; $4 \to 3$ cts at $P = 4$) — **`-25%` download, Stage 1 DB RAM/MACs, Stage 2 packing, and online server latency**.
  - **`3,104 B` rows (`d_row = 1552` words, $C = 1$) at $P = 166$**: Cuts response ciphertexts from $166 \to \lceil 166 \cdot 1552 / 2048 \rceil = 126$ (**`-24.1%`** download and Stage 2 packing, **`-24.2%`** Stage 1 MACs and condensed DB RAM).

### Tall-Colour Multi-Sample $t = 1$ Selectors & Unified Block Pipeline (`Gen 4 R1` — `crypto/pir/pir_params.h`, `crypto/pir/client/pir_client.cc`, `crypto/pir/server/preprocessing_server.h`, `crypto/pir/server/pir_server.cc`)

When a colour table exceeds the ring degree ($R_i > d = 2048$), `R1` extends one-dimensional $t = 1$ retrieval to arbitrary heights without increasing response ciphertexts or Stage 2 LWE-to-RLWE packing work:

- **Multi-sample $t = 1$ selector groups (`BuildSelectorGroupPlans`, `PirClient::CreateGroupedSelector`)**:
  - `BuildSelectorGroupPlans` (`crypto/pir/pir_params.h:258-290`) greedily packs short colours ($\sum R_i \le 2048$) into shared single-sample groups (`num_samples = 1`) and assigns any tall colour ($R_i > 2048$) its own multi-sample group with `num_samples = ceil(R_i / 2048)` independent CRS polynomials $(a_{i,0}, \dots, a_{i,m-1})$ encrypted under the **same** per-query secret key $s(X)$.
  - Only the unique sample $\lfloor r_i^* / 2048 \rfloor$ encrypts $\Delta X^{r_i^* \bmod 2048}$; the remaining `num_samples - 1` samples encrypt $0$, and only the first $R_i$ coefficients are modulus-switched and serialized onto the wire.
- **Unified continuous cross-colour block layout (`PackedRowWords`, `PreprocessCrossColorStripe`)**:
  - Defining `d_row = PackedRowWords(row_u16, 2048) = row_u16` unconditionally for all `row_u16 >= 1`, sub-tile ($d_{\text{row}} < 2048$), exact-tile ($2048 \mid d_{\text{row}}$), and multi-tile ($d_{\text{row}} > 2048$) workloads all route through a single continuous `N_resp = ceil(P * d_row / 2048)` cross-colour block pipeline (`PreprocessCrossColorStripe` + `PirServer::CreateCrossColor`).
  - Stage 1 contracts each $2048$-row block against its corresponding CRS negacyclic matrix, while Stage 2 still packs only $N_{\text{resp}}$ blocks of $2048$ LWEs—independent of $R_i$.

### Centered Signed `int16` Contraction & Analytical `40/42`-Bit Selector Scaling (`Gen 4 R1a + R1b` — `crypto/pir/pir_params.h`, `crypto/pir/server/preprocessing_server.h`, `crypto/pir/server/pir_server.cc`)

At $R_i \gg 2048$, uncentered unsigned `uint16_t` entries $[0, 2^{16}-1]$ amplify both fresh selector binomial error ($\sigma^2 = 40$) and 40-bit selector rounding error ($\delta_r \in [-2^{11}, 2^{11}]$) by $\mathbb{E}[D^2] \approx 2^{32}/3$ (and up to $(2^{16}-1)^2 \approx 2^{32}$ on constant `0xFFFF` payloads):

- **Centered signed `int16` Stage 1 contraction (`centered_db`, `R1a`)**:
  - For tall tables (`max_rows > 2048` by default under `--centered_db=auto`), database entries are interpreted as signed integers $D_{\text{signed}} = (D \oplus 2^{15}) - 2^{15} \in [-2^{15}, 2^{15}-1]$ with $D_{\text{signed}} \equiv D \pmod{2^{16}}$.
  - Passing $D \oplus \text{0x8000}$ into `Matrix<uint16_t>::Condense` (which subtracts $2^{15}$ internally) stores $D_{\text{signed}}$ directly in the 16-lane AVX2 `vpmaddwd` matrix, omits the online $+2^{15}\sum_r v_r$ bias restoration in `MultiplyStripe`, and subtracts $2^{15}\sum_{r=0}^{R_i-1} \text{row}_r(\text{Negacyclic}(a_i)) \pmod{2^{52}}$ once from the offline LWE hint.
  - Cuts worst-case Stage 1 payload norm squared by **$4\times$** ($2^{30}$ vs $2^{32}$) and random-payload variance by **$4\times$** ($2^{30}/3$ vs $2^{32}/3$), with zero online instructions added.
- **Analytical `40/42`-bit `<3, 10>` selector modulus switching (`AutoSelectorBits`, `R1b`)**:
  - The 3-digit balanced 14-bit AVX2 `vpmaddwd` kernel (`crypto/matrix.cc`) natively accepts up to $3 \times 14 = 42$ selector bits (`shift = 52 - 42 = 10`) with **zero kernel changes or 64-bit widening**.
  - `AutoSelectorBits(max_phys_rows)` (`crypto/pir/pir_params.h:295`) returns `40` (`5.0 B/row`) for `max_phys_rows <= 8192` and `42` (`5.25 B/row`, $+5\%$ upload) for `max_phys_rows > 8192`, reducing selector-rounding variance by **$16\times$** ($\text{Var}(\delta_r): 2^{24}/12 \to 2^{20}/12$) and maintaining **`4.75–5.94σ` decoding margin** ($\sigma_{q_1} \approx 43.1\text{--}53.9 \ll 256$ even unbundled; `5.40–5.91σ` wire-optimal) through $R_i = 131{,}072$.

### Contiguous Plaintext Row Bundling: Compute-Optimal Default (`--bundle_rows=1`) & Wire-Optimal Mode (`--bundle_rows=0`, `Gen 4 R2` — `crypto/pir/pir_params.h`, `crypto/pir/benchmarking/tiptoe_workload.cc`)

Under pure unbundled $t = 1$ (`R1`), upload grows linearly with $\sum_i R_i$ (`5.0–5.25 B/row`), dominating total communication once $M \gg 2048$. Instead of homomorphic second-dimension RGSW folding ($t = 2$, which adds a `42.25 KiB` RGSW ciphertext and $P \cdot C$ expensive online `ExternalProduct` FFTs while keeping $100\%$ of Stage 1 work and doubling Stage 2 packing), `R2` folds $B_{\text{row}}$ consecutive logical rows into a single wider physical row **in plaintext**:

- **Plaintext row folding (`BundleRows` in `crypto/pir/pir_params.h`)**:
  - Given logical row width $w_{16} = \lceil \text{row\_bytes} / 2 \rceil$ `uint16_t` words and bundle factor $B_{\text{row}} \in [1, 64]$, `BundleRows` packs logical rows $[q B_{\text{row}}, \min((q+1)B_{\text{row}}, R_i))$ contiguously into physical row $q \in [0, \lceil R_i / B_{\text{row}} \rceil)$ of active width $d_{\text{row}} = B_{\text{row}} \cdot w_{16}$ words (`phys_c = ceil(B_row * w_16 / 2048)`).
  - The client queries physical row $\lfloor r_i^* / B_{\text{row}} \rfloor$ and slices words $[(r_i^* \bmod B_{\text{row}}) w_{16},\ +w_{16})$ after decryption.
- **`--bundle_rows` control modes & compute-optimal default (`1`)**:
  - **`--bundle_rows=1` (Default — Compute-Optimal Unbundled Mode)**: Leaves tables unbundled ($B_{\text{row}} = 1$), minimizing $N_{\text{resp}} = \lceil P \cdot w_{16} / 2048 \rceil$ and therefore minimizing online Stage 2 `LWEsToRLWE` server CPU work, offline packing setup, and client decryptions.
  - **`--bundle_rows=0` (Wire-Optimal `ChooseBundleFactor` Mode)**: Invokes `ChooseBundleFactor(rows, row_u16, selector_bits)` to select the discrete $B_{\text{row}} \in [1, 64]$ minimizing total batch wire bytes (`1` whenever $\max_i R_i \le 2048$, ties broken toward fewer response blocks and smaller $B_{\text{row}}$).
  - **`--bundle_rows=B` ($B \ge 2$ — Explicit Pareto Frontier Mode)**: Explicitly bundles $B$ logical rows per physical row to trade Stage 2 server compute against upload bandwidth along the Pareto frontier.
- **Exact hardware-free wire objective (`BundledWireBytes`, `ChooseBundleFactor`)**:
  - For any candidate $B_{\text{row}} \in [1, \min(64, M_{\max})]$, physical heights are $M_{i,\text{phys}} = \lceil R_i / B_{\text{row}} \rceil$, selector width is $b_{\text{sel}} = \text{AutoSelectorBits}(\max_i M_{i,\text{phys}})$, `PackedRowWords(B_row * w_16, 2048) = B_row * w_16`, and exact batch wire bytes are:
    $$\text{Wire}(B_{\text{row}}) = 53{,}248 + \sum_{i=0}^{P-1} \left\lceil \frac{M_{i,\text{phys}} \cdot b_{\text{sel}}}{8} \right\rceil + 11{,}008 \cdot \left\lceil \frac{P \cdot B_{\text{row}} \cdot w_{16}}{2048} \right\rceil.$$
  - Because continuous cross-colour packing packs $P \cdot B_{\text{row}} \cdot w_{16}$ words across colours without whole-tile rounding for any $B_{\text{row}} \cdot w_{16} \ge 1$, intermediate and odd bundle factors never suffer partial-tile padding waste (e.g., on `W2a_M131k` at $P = 4, M = 131{,}072, w_{16} = 1024$, $B_{\text{row}} = 11$ packs into $N_{\text{resp}} = 22$ blocks and `532.9 KiB` vs $B_{\text{row}} = 12$ at $24$ blocks and `534.0 KiB`).
- **Asymptotic & empirical wire impact (under `--bundle_rows=0` wire-optimal mode)**:
  - Total Stage 1 database entries $M_{\text{phys}} \cdot (B_{\text{row}} w_{16}) \approx R_i \cdot w_{16}$ stay constant while upload and download balance to $\mathcal{O}(\sqrt{M \cdot P \cdot w_{16}})$, cutting total communication vs $M = 2048$ partitioned baselines by **4.03×** on `W1` (`630.0 → 156.5 KiB` at $M=65{,}536, P=1$, or **4.58×** vs pre-continuous-packing `716.0 KiB`), **7.48×** on `W2a` (`3,988.0 → 532.9 KiB` at $M=131{,}072, P=4$), and **7.99×** on `W2b` (`8,116.0 → 1,016.0 KiB` at $M=131{,}072, P=4$).
  - At $B_{\text{row}} = 2$, plaintext bundling costs $+11{,}008 \cdot (\lceil 2 P w_{16} / 2048 \rceil - \lceil P w_{16} / 2048 \rceil)$ download bytes vs $+43{,}264$ RGSW upload bytes for homomorphic $t = 2$, so $B_{\text{row}} = 2$ is strictly smaller on wire whenever $P \cdot C \le 3$ (and at $P \ge 4$ whenever $C \le 7$, i.e. payloads $\le 28\text{ KiB}$, once $B_{\text{row}} \ge 2$ amortizes across colours) while eliminating $100\%$ of online RGSW `ExternalProduct` work.
- **Stage 2 server CPU vs wire caveat (why `--bundle_rows=1` is default)**:
  - Stage 1 scalar MACs ($P \cdot \lceil R_i / B_{\text{row}} \rceil \cdot (B_{\text{row}} \cdot w_{16}) \approx P \cdot R_i \cdot w_{16}$) are **invariant** to $B_{\text{row}}$, whereas Stage 2 `LWEsToRLWE` packing (~70–75% of online server CPU time) scales **linearly** with $N_{\text{resp}} = \lceil P \cdot B_{\text{row}} \cdot w_{16} / 2048 \rceil \propto B_{\text{row}}$. Because `ChooseBundleFactor` (`--bundle_rows=0`) optimizes pure wire $\text{Upload}(B_{\text{row}}) + \text{Download}(B_{\text{row}}) \approx U / B_{\text{row}} + D \cdot B_{\text{row}}$ with zero weight on Stage 2 server CPU — and that wire hyperbola has a flat minimum around $B_{\text{row}}^* = \sqrt{U / D}$ — continuous cross-colour packing lets intermediate/odd bundle factors shave a small fraction of wire at a steep Stage 2 CPU cost (see **Assumptions, Caveats & Tradeoffs** below).

## Assumptions, Caveats & Tradeoffs

- **Bandwidth vs Stage 2 server CPU tradeoff (`--bundle_rows=1` default vs `--bundle_rows=0` `ChooseBundleFactor`)**:
  - Online Stage 1 database contraction performs $P \cdot \lceil R_i / B_{\text{row}} \rceil \cdot (B_{\text{row}} \cdot w_{16}) \approx P \cdot R_i \cdot w_{16}$ scalar MACs regardless of $B_{\text{row}}$, whereas Stage 2 `LWEsToRLWE` packing (`pack_matrix` + `pack_finalize` + `modswitch`, which accounts for **~70–75% of online server CPU time**) scales **linearly** with the response ciphertext count $N_{\text{resp}} = \lceil P \cdot B_{\text{row}} \cdot w_{16} / 2048 \rceil \propto B_{\text{row}}$.
  - `ChooseBundleFactor` (`--bundle_rows=0`) minimizes unweighted total wire $\text{Upload}(B_{\text{row}}) + \text{Download}(B_{\text{row}}) \approx U / B_{\text{row}} + D \cdot B_{\text{row}}$, whose objective curve is flat near $B_{\text{row}}^* = \sqrt{U / D}$. With continuous cross-colour packing enabled across all widths, intermediate or odd bundle factors can slightly reduce total wire while substantially increasing $N_{\text{resp}}$ and server latency: for example, on `W2a_M8192_P64_W2048` ($P = 64, R_i = 8{,}192, w_{16} = 1024$), moving from $B_{\text{row}} = 2 \to 3$ trims total wire by only **`-4.1%`** (`2,020.0 → 1,937.4 KiB`) at the expense of **`+50%` more response ciphertexts** (`64 → 96` cts) and **`~+28%` slower online server latency**.
  - Consequently, **`--bundle_rows=1` is the default** (compute-optimal unbundled mode) so workloads never incur unexpected Stage 2 server slowdowns, and callers sweep or set `--bundle_rows` (`1` for min-CPU, `2` or `4` for balanced knee points, or `0` for strict min-wire) explicitly according to their network-bandwidth vs server-compute budget.

- **Arbitrary table row counts & row widths ($R_i \ge 1, d_{\text{row}} \ge 1$, verified through $R_i = 131{,}072$)**: Colour table row counts $R_i$ do **not** need to be powers of two, multiples of the ring degree, or $\le 2048$, and active row widths $d_{\text{row}}$ do **not** need to be $\le 1024$ or multiples of $2048$. Continuous cross-colour packing (`PackedRowWords`), multi-sample $t = 1$ selectors (`R1`), centered `int16` contraction + `40/42`-bit selector scaling (`R1a/R1b`), and plaintext row bundling (`R2`) handle arbitrary $(R_i, d_{\text{row}})$ seamlessly (`40`-bit selectors for physical heights $\le 8{,}192$, `42`-bit selectors above $8{,}192$, and 32-row SIMD contraction granularity).

- **Precomputed FFT & mask memory (`Gen 1 I3a`, `Gen 2 I1b`, `Gen 3 S4b/c`)**: With length-$d$ negacyclic FFTs (`S4c`), `PrecomputeTVecHFfts` uses `256 KiB` per column (`50%` less than Gen 2), `a_mod_switched_t1_` uses `16 KiB` per column, and `a_mod_switched_t1_packed_` uses `6.25 KiB` per column. Note: Under glibc's default arena allocator, long-lived allocations after multi-threaded preprocessing can retain freed thread arenas in RSS; `tcmalloc`/`jemalloc` avoids this.
- **Exactness budget (`Gen 1 I4`, `Gen 2 I2b`, `Gen 3 S4c`)**: `kFftExactProductBits = 44` is a conservative analytic bound ($53 - 8 - 1$) that holds with zero extra chunking at $d=2048$; `allow_paired_ifft` is guarded by $\log_2(N_{\text{polys}}) + \log_2 d + u_{\text{mag}} + v_{\text{mag}} \le 50.0$ (RMS error $\sim 0.0055 \ll 0.5$).
- **Single-threaded execution per `PirServer` / `PirClient` instance (`Gen 1 I4`, `Gen 2 I2d`)**: Scratch buffers in `fft_ctx_` are reused across calls on the same instance; concurrent queries sharing a single `PirServer` shard should mark `fft_ctx_` `thread_local`. The threaded Tiptoe adapter avoids this by giving every worker its own servers.
- **Hardware generalization**: Matrix kernels (`I5, I3b, I3, I4`) use Google Highway SIMD, while `S3b` (`MultiplyCondensedMatrixVector<uint16_t>`) and `I2c` (`crypto/fft.h`) include `#if defined(__AVX2__)` fast paths with clean portable Highway/scalar `#else` fallbacks.
